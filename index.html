About This Project

This project investigates the relationship between reaction rate and three important factors: temperature, concentration, and surface area.

Research Question

How do temperature, concentration, and surface area affect the rate of a chemical reaction?

Hypothesis

Increasing temperature, concentration, or surface area is expected to increase reaction rate under comparable conditions.

💡 Scientific Background

Collision Theory

Collision theory states that reacting particles must collide with sufficient energy and an appropriate orientation for a chemical reaction to occur.

Temperature

Increasing temperature increases the average kinetic energy of particles. This can increase the frequency of effective collisions and the fraction of particles able to overcome the activation energy barrier.

Concentration

Increasing concentration generally means more reacting particles are present in a given volume, increasing opportunities for collisions.

Surface Area

For a solid reactant, increasing exposed surface area can provide more opportunities for collisions with other reactant particles.

📖 Discussion

All three illustrative datasets show a positive relationship between the investigated variable and reaction rate.

The temperature trend is consistent with collision theory because higher temperature increases particle kinetic energy and can increase the number of collisions with sufficient energy.

The concentration trend is consistent with increased collision opportunities when more particles occupy the same volume.

The surface-area trend is consistent with increased exposure of a solid reactant to collisions with other particles.

⚠️ Limitations

  • No laboratory experiment was conducted.
  • The datasets are illustrative rather than experimental.
  • No experimental uncertainty was calculated.
  • No repeated laboratory trials were performed.
  • The three datasets use different scales and conditions.
