About the Project

This project investigates how temperature, concentration, and surface area can affect the rate of a chemical reaction. The interpretation is based on collision theory.

Research Integrity: The datasets used in this website are illustrative. They were not obtained from laboratory measurements.

Research Question

How do temperature, concentration, and surface area affect the rate of a chemical reaction?

Hypothesis

Increasing temperature, concentration, or surface area is expected to increase reaction rate under comparable conditions.

Scientific Explanation

🌡️ Temperature

Higher temperature increases the average kinetic energy of particles. More particles can therefore participate in effective collisions.

🧪 Concentration

Increasing concentration generally increases the number of reacting particles in a given volume, increasing collision opportunities.

⚗️ Surface Area

Increasing the exposed surface area of a solid reactant can increase opportunities for collisions with other reactant particles.

💡 Collision Theory

Chemical reactions require effective collisions. Particles must collide with sufficient energy and suitable orientation for a reaction to occur.

Conclusion

The illustrative datasets show increasing reaction rates as temperature, concentration, and surface area increase. These trends are consistent with the qualitative predictions of collision theory.

However, because the data are illustrative rather than experimental measurements, the results should not be treated as laboratory evidence.

Future Work

  • Estimate experimental uncertainty.
